Deep Autumn is warm-neutral with depth. Your colours are rich and grounded, carrying warmth without being exclusively warm. If it looks icy, chalky, pastel, or high-contrast, it is not yours. If it looks deep, warm-neutral, and richly alive, you are in the right place.

This is inspiration, not rules. Every colour in your palette works with you and with each other. Use this guide as a reference and a starting point as you learn your colours. Colour analysis is about empowerment, not restriction. Take what feels right, when it feels right, and leave what doesn't.

The Deep Autumn palette contains thousands of colours. The swatches here are carefully chosen examples to help you understand the qualities to look for, deep, warm-neutral, and richly grounded. When you shop, use these as a visual compass. Deep Autumn flows into Winter, which means you can borrow some deeper, darker Winter tones. Keep them warm-neutral or slightly muted and they will work beautifully.

Your essence: Strong. Elegant. Mature. Powerful. Credible. Authentic. These words describe the energy your palette carries. This guide gives you permission to step into your full depth and wear it with confidence.

How to use this palette as a range guide: Your palette spans from warm-neutral camels and sandstone tones all the way through to deep espresso, warm black, and rich oxblood, and all of it works for you. Use the lightest swatches as your guide to how light you can go while keeping that deep, warm quality. Use the darkest swatches as your guide to how rich and dark you can go. Everything in between is your sweet spot. When you are shopping and unsure about a shade, find the closest colour in your palette and use it as your reference point.

Accessories & Eyewear

The bigger picture: Every colour in your palette can be referenced for accessories. A deep oxblood bag, a bark brown belt, a warm garnet nail, it all works. The guidance below helps you understand why certain choices feel more harmonious, so you can shop with confidence rather than follow a checklist.

Tortoiseshell and dark warm brown are your standout frame choices, they echo your natural depth and look effortlessly harmonious. Warm black, olive, and deep oxblood are equally strong. Avoid: bright silver, clear frames, icy chrome, or any cool grey tone.
Lens tints for sunglasses
Warm amber, deep brown, or bronze lenses are your most harmonious options. Dark olive-green tinted lenses also work beautifully. A warm copper or dark amber mirror lens is a great statement choice for Deep Autumn.

Scarves, Hats & Wraps
Near-face accessories matter most
Anything worn close to your face, scarves, hat brims, collars, has the same impact as a top or blouse. Use your Near The Face colours as a guide here. A deep teal scarf, a warm burgundy hat, or a forest green wrap near your face will all be beautifully flattering. Tip: a rich, warm-toned scarf near the face can transform an otherwise neutral outfit into something that truly works for your season.

For the Men
Ties, pocket squares & accessories

A deep teal or warm wine tie over grey or cool black.
Makeup & Hair
Your Deep Autumn guide to beauty. Deep, warm, and richly grounded. Shades listed are longstanding classics worth looking for.

Your golden rule: If it looks cool, icy, or pastel, put it back. Deep Autumn makeup is warm, deep, and intentional. A rich oxblood or warm garnet lip with minimal eye makeup will always look more "you" than a full cool face. Your depth and warmth do the work, let your colours support that.
Lips

Your "nude" lip — warm and deep, not cool beige
Cool or pale nudes wash you out completely. Your version of a no-makeup lip is a warm nude, deep caramel, or sheer warm brown. Look for shades described as warm nude, warm beige, or caramel. Try: ILIA Balmy Tint in a warm peachy-brown tone, or a warm brown lip liner worn alone for a natural everyday look.
Blush
Warm terracottas, deep peaches, and earthy corals bring life to your face. Blush should look like a warm, rich flush, not pink or cool. A light touch of warm bronzer can double beautifully as blush for Deep Autumn.

Look for warm terracotta, deep peach, earthy coral, or warm brick blush. Avoid cool pink, lavender-rose, or berry-toned blush, these work against your warm-neutral colouring rather than enhancing it. Tip: a warm bronzer swept lightly on the cheeks is a natural, foolproof go-to for Deep Autumn, it adds warmth and depth in one step and works across all skin tones.
Foundation & Tinted Moisturiser
Your most important foundation rule: warm or neutral-warm undertone. Golden or peachy-based foundations work best. Pink-based or cool foundations will look ashy and fight your natural depth.
What to look for
Ask for golden, warm, or peachy undertone in your shade. Avoid shades described as pink, cool, or neutral-cool. Deep Autumn often has medium to deep skin, so finding a shade with warm depth is important. Tip: when testing foundation, a warm match that looks alive and rich is right. One that looks ashy, grey, or pink-toned is too cool for you.
Eyes
Your eyes thrive with warm depth and rich definition. Bronze, copper, and deep forest tones are deeply flattering. The warm brown palette was made for you, lean into it fully.
Eyeshadow — what works
Base shades: warm ivory, golden beige, soft peach, light caramel. Depth: deep chocolate, warm espresso, dark olive, burnt umber, forest green. Accents: bronze, copper, warm gold, deep teal, smoked paprika. Rich metallic and satin finishes look incredible on Deep Autumn. Avoid: cool grey, silver, icy lavender, stark black, or any cool metallic.
Eyeliner
Warm dark brown or espresso is your most flattering liner. If you want more drama, try warm black, deep chocolate, or deep forest green. Avoid cool grey or blue-black liner, which creates a harsh coolness that fights your warm-neutral depth. Tip: a deep warm brown liner with a brick red lip is a quintessential Deep Autumn look.
Mascara
Warm brown or dark espresso mascara is your most flattering choice. Warm black works beautifully too. Pure cool black can work but may feel stark, if you use it, pair with softer warm eye makeup elsewhere. Try: any brown-black shade for a rich, natural finish that enhances rather than harshens.
Brows & Beard
Brow guidance
Match your brow product to your natural hair colour, or go one shade warmer and slightly deeper. A warm dark brown or deep espresso brow product works beautifully. Avoid anything with ash, cool, or grey undertones. Tip: ask for "warm brown" or "dark warm brown" when shopping brow pencils or powders.
Beard & grooming colour
Facial hair looks excellent in this season, the natural depth and warmth of Deep Autumn colouring suits well-kept facial hair beautifully. If you colour your beard or brows, look for deep warm brown, espresso, or warm black shades. Avoid anything with ash, cool, or blue-black tones. Tip: keep lines clean and intentional, Deep Autumn suits structured grooming that feels deliberate.
Hair Colour
Your most beautiful hair colour is often the one you were born with. Deep Autumn's natural colouring tends toward deep, rich, warm-toned hair, and that is exactly what flatters you most.
Your best hair colours
Deep and warm: deep brown, espresso, warm black, and auburn-brown are all stunning. These shades echo your natural depth and colouring. Avoid: ashy tones, platinum or icy highlights, cool black, or anything that introduces coolness or lightness to your hair, these work against your natural richness.
Highlights & colour techniques
If you highlight, choose deep warm caramel, auburn, or copper tones only. Keep them subtle and close in depth to your base, Deep Autumn does not suit high-contrast highlighting. Avoid: cool ash highlights, platinum balayage, or any technique that introduces icy or light tones. Depth and warmth are your strengths.
What to ask your colourist
Ask for "warm toned," "no ash," and "deep" for your shade. Show your colourist your palette, having your actual colour reference makes the conversation much easier. Tip: when in doubt, go deeper and warmer rather than lighter or cooler.
Shopping tip: When you find a product you love but are not sure if it works, try asking an AI assistant: "Is [Brand Name] [Product Name] in [Shade Name] warm or cool toned? Is it suitable for Deep Autumn?" Then do a visual check by comparing the product swatch against your palette. Trust your eye.
